  • R2 Research

    Include items that report a **primary research artifact**: a peer-reviewed or preprint paper, an official technical report from a research lab, or a new public benchmark **with a link to the paper/report/benchmark page itself**. Exclude marketing benchmark scores, blog summaries without the original paper/report link, and product announcements.

    Propose a change

  • R3 Industry

    Include items that report **AI industry business events**: financing, M&A, partnerships, market/revenue data, or non-model product launches, with a link to a company announcement, deal announcement, or reputable media report. If the item is a model/capability release **with an official owner announcement**, use R1 instead; if it is open-source tooling, use R5. Media-only coverage of a model launch (no official owner link) stays under R3.

    Propose a change

  • R4 Policy

    Include items that report **AI policy, regulation, enforcement, or official compliance actions** by governments, courts, standards bodies, or regulators, with a link to the official document or to reputable coverage of that event. Exclude opinion pieces and general ethics commentary without a concrete official action or rule.

    Propose a change

  • R5 Tools

    Include items that report the **release or major update of an open-source AI developer project** (libraries, agents, evals, infra) and link to the repository, release page, or official maintainer write-up. "Notable usage" counts only when the primary link is that OSS project's repo/release/docs—not a cloud vendor marketing post about running someone else's tool.

About · CANON

What this is — The first live CANON instance (github.com/larrymou/canon): constituents legislate inclusion rules on this repo, an AI editor applies only ratified rules, and GitHub publishes one shared 5-day window for everyone. Not a recommender.

Why — Recommendation feeds optimize clicks and quietly build filter bubbles. AICanonFeed sits in the opposite seat: rules instead of personalization, a shared record in decisions/ instead of a black box. The product is designed forward — rules and quotas evolve; we do not rewrite history to match.

CANON (paradigm: github.com/larrymou/canon) in three steps here — community legislates on Issues, AI judges only by enacted rules, and GitHub (code + history) is the neutral executor.

01
Legislate
Open a rule proposal via GitHub Issues. Bound by meta-rules M1–M7.
02
Adjudicate
AI pre-reviews proposals and applies only ratified rules in rules/ — it never invents inclusion criteria.
03
Execute
Votes, PRs, and decisions/ are public. No ranking, no pins — every inclusion can be audited.

How to join — Propose a rule, vote 👍 / 👎 on issues labeled voting, or audit calls under decisions/. Full guide: CONTRIBUTING.md. Rules take ~8–14 days.

  • Strict reverse-chronological order. Tabs are filters, not rankings.
  • Research is capped on ingest and again on the page so industry/policy/tools stay visible.
  • Uncovered items are rejected by design.
  • Meta-rule M7 forbids personalization: no per-user feeds, no engagement ranking.
  • Rules change over time; past decisions keep the rule version in effect when they were made.
